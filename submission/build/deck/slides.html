Manal · 0:25–1:05. Here is the problem. A school issues a diploma on Hedera and later revokes it. Consensus is final, the revocation is on the ledger. But a recruiter's app never talks to consensus: it reads through one mirror node, a REST API that returns plain JSON with no proof. If that one mirror is compromised, it can delete the revocation and re-forge the hash chain, serve an old snapshot, or show different clients different stories. The recruiter then accepts a revoked diploma. Over to Aymane for our solution.

03 · The solution

Several mirrors. One truth.

01 · VERIFY

Quorum + cryptography

Ask several mirrors, recompute Hedera's SHA-384 running hash, check it ends at the consensus-node anchor.

02 · EXPLAIN

AI forensic report

Which mirror lied, how, at which sequence numbers, and what to do about it.

03 · PROVE

Audit trail on HCS

Every verdict and its report hash are attested on the Hedera Consensus Service.

Golden ruleThe AI proposes. Deterministic logic and cryptography decide.
Aymane · 1:05–1:45. HashWitness does three things. First, it verifies: it asks several mirrors, recomputes Hedera's own SHA-384 running-hash chain locally, and checks that the chain ends exactly at the hash a consensus node reports. A forged history cannot. Second, it explains: an AI agent writes a forensic report. Third, it proves: every verdict is attested on the Hedera Consensus Service. And one golden rule: the AI proposes, cryptography decides. No verdict ever depends on the AI.

Redone · 2:45–3:35. Why Hedera? Three reasons, all used in the code. One: the Consensus Service gives every message a running hash that commits to the whole history. We reimplemented Hedera's exact algorithm and matched 100 out of 100 real testnet messages, so a mirror cannot alter or drop a message unnoticed. Two: we read the topic head straight from a consensus node. That anchor turns a majority vote into a proof: a forged chain, even a perfect-looking one, does not end at that hash. Three: every verification is attested on HCS, a public, ordered audit log, for about 0.0053 hbar each. Mirrors are the data source; two are real, the attacker is simulated. The AI agent runs live on OpenRouter with a rule-based fallback, and a Thompson-sampling bandit picks mirrors to cut query cost.

The numbers are measured: zero lies accepted, in every testnet attack and in 200 simulated scenarios; 77 percent lower query cost with adaptive mirror selection, in simulation; three out of three attacks caught on real testnet data. 07 · Vision

Don't trust the mirror. Verify it.

What's different

  • Cryptographic anchor, not a majority vote
  • Works with today's mirror APIs
  • AI that explains, never decides

Honest limits

  • 2 real mirrors, attacker simulated
  • Anchor from one consensus node
  • Mirror lag can look like replay

Next

  • Anchor from several consensus nodes
